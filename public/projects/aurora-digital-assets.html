Aurora Digital Assets

Institutional control layer for regulated digital asset operations.

Aurora is a deterministic prototype for risk review, compliance workflows, security guardrails, evidence packaging and human review before sensitive digital asset operations can proceed.

View architecture Start a conversation

Aurora is not a wallet. Aurora is a control layer.

The system is designed to sit between a regulated institution, compliance teams, risk operations and future regulated custodians. Its purpose is not to custody assets, but to make operations auditable, reviewable and controllable before execution.

What Aurora is

A bank-facing control layer for digital asset operations, approvals, evidence and review workflows.

What Aurora is not

Not a wallet, not an exchange, not a custodian and not a live banking integration.

Prototype boundary

Local deterministic backend prototype. No real assets, no real custody execution and no private keys.

Control architecture

Built around review before execution.

Aurora models the institutional controls required before regulated digital asset operations, cloud deployment paths or custodian integrations are allowed to proceed.

Core modules

Deterministic guardrails for high-trust workflows.

AI Threat Defense

Models defensive review flows for AI-assisted risk signals and suspicious operation chains.

Secret Exposure Guard

Prevents sensitive workflows from proceeding when secret exposure risks are active.

Sensitive Approval Guard

Separates customer approval from backend risk approval for sensitive operations.

Cloud / Kubernetes Guardrails

Blocks future deployment paths when infrastructure controls are not verified.

Custodian Integration Guardrails

Evaluates regulated custodian readiness, boundaries, liabilities and control gaps.

Evidence & Review Lifecycle

Turns risk signals into evidence packages, review cases and auditable lifecycle transitions.

Public safety boundary

Designed as a prototype, not as a live custody system.

Aurora does not custody assets, move funds, store private keys, connect to live banks, connect to exchanges, connect to cloud providers or integrate with real custodians. The prototype models institutional control logic before those integrations can safely exist.
