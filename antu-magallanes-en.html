01 / VISION

Scientific infrastructure conceived as an ecosystem

Antü Magallanes explores the possibility of establishing an advanced scientific-computing and AI ecosystem in southern Chile, connecting national capability, polar research, responsible energy and international cooperation.

The initiative extends beyond a data center. It could bring together high-performance computing, sovereign AI infrastructure, scientific cloud services, Antarctic and polar data platforms, advanced training, innovation and regional industrial development. A future Antü Magallanes Supercomputing Center would be a possible central pillar—not the entire vision.

This is a preliminary initiative. Every capability, location, partnership, scale and institutional model remains subject to technical, environmental, energy, economic, legal and scientific-demand assessment.

02 / CONTEXT

Why this conversation matters now

Demand for AI and scientific computing is growing as expectations for trust, resilience and environmental performance become more exacting.

Scientific capacity

Climate modelling, Earth observation, computational biology and other disciplines increasingly rely on large-scale data processing.

Trusted infrastructure

Governments and institutions need verifiable control over data, identities, models, digital supply chains and continuity.

Energy reality

Advanced computing requires energy, cooling and resilient networks. Expansion must be assessed together with its impacts.

Competitive cooperation

Countries are strengthening AI and HPC capability. Strategic autonomy can coexist with open science and international work.

03 / CHILE

Chile as a scientific bridge from the Pacific to Antarctica

Chile offers the potential to connect South America, the Pacific and Antarctica, building on its traditions in astronomy, polar research, Earth observation and natural sciences.

Experience hosting international research infrastructure, renewable-energy potential, mineral and industrial knowledge, and growing digital connectivity could support national AI and HPC capability with Latin American reach. Institutional continuity and durable agreements would matter as much as technology.

These potential advantages must be tested against evidence on demand, energy, connectivity, permitting, cost and national capability. Geography alone does not justify investment.

04 / TERRITORY

Why explore Magallanes

The region combines Antarctic relevance, scientific identity, a southern climate and potential clean-energy ecosystems—but its constraints require rigorous examination.

Antarctic gateway

Its scientific and logistical links to Antarctica could support polar research, Southern Ocean work and southern-hemisphere data.

Climate and cooling

Temperatures may support some cooling approaches. Actual efficiency depends on design, site, humidity, water and operations.

Future energy

Wind resources and possible clean-energy ecosystems warrant assessment without assuming availability, cost, additionality or resilience.

Regional capability

Logistics, suppliers, technical education and industrial capacity could develop with the initiative; readiness must be measured.

Diversification

Science and digital services could broaden the regional economy if value, talent and decision-making include the region.

Critical conditions

Energy, water, redundant connectivity, construction, environmental effects and operational continuity need independent studies.

A cold climate does not automatically make a data center sustainable. Performance must be demonstrated across the full lifecycle.

07 / TRUST

Digital sovereignty beyond servers

Sovereign infrastructure requires verifiable institutional control over critical data, models, identities, permissions and services.

  • Data governance: classification, residency, authorised use and retention.
  • Identity and access: least privilege, trusted federation and accountability.
  • Provenance: traceability of models, data, software and outputs.
  • Supply chain: integrity of components, dependencies and updates.
  • Evidence and audit: demonstrable decisions and operations.
  • Research environments: secure spaces for sensitive data.
  • Continuity: recovery, redundancy and operational control.
  • Responsible AI: risk assessment, human oversight and legitimate use.

This direction connects carefully with CAUCO’s broader trust-infrastructure vision: trust built into architecture, without making Antü Magallanes a commercial showcase.

08 / RESPONSIBILITY

Energy and environment as decision criteria

The initiative’s legitimacy would depend on public evidence of energy and environmental performance—not assumed geographic advantages.

Energy baseline

Assess source, additionality, price, emissions, grid capacity, redundancy and backup.

Efficiency and scale

Set verifiable targets, phased capacity and utilisation thresholds before expansion.

Cooling and water

Compare alternatives and measure water use under local scenarios.

Heat and territory

Study heat reuse, construction, biodiversity, noise and territorial relationships.

Lifecycle

Account for embodied and operational carbon, equipment, replacement and disposal.

Transparency

Link permitting, methodology and periodic reporting to auditable indicators.

Environmental and energy performance must be demonstrated through independent feasibility studies, applicable permits and transparent monitoring.

09 / REGION

Regional capability, not an enclave

Antü Magallanes would create lasting value only if it builds capability in the region—not merely equipment.

Over time, it could support specialist employment, training and reskilling, university and technical programmes, local suppliers, technology transfer, startups, scientific conferences, and exports of digital and research services.

Regional participation should begin during feasibility. Educational institutions, communities, authorities, businesses and territorial organisations would need a role in priorities, impacts and benefit mechanisms. No employment or investment figures are projected without independent evidence.

10 / WORLD

International cooperation from the far south

Antü Magallanes could provide a neutral platform for shared science, capacity access, training and responsible technology development.

Potential areas for exploratory cooperation

  • Chile and Latin America
  • Finland, the Nordic region and European Union
  • China, Japan and South Korea
  • United Arab Emirates
  • North America
  • International Antarctic research networks

Strategic outreach directions, not confirmed partnerships.

11 / GOVERNANCE

An institution still to be designed

Governance must protect scientific independence, Chilean strategic interests, regional participation, transparency and long-term continuity.

One option to study is an independent foundation or nonprofit entity involving Chilean public institutions, regional representation, universities and scientific bodies. It could include technical and scientific boards, ethics and sustainability oversight, public-interest mechanisms, responsible industry participation and an international advisory council.

No model has been chosen. Any structure would require transparent rules for access, procurement, conflicts, audit, security, intellectual property and accountability.

12 / DELIVERY

Grow only when evidence supports it

A gated route would allow the initiative to stop, adapt or expand at explicit decisions—without fictional dates.

Phase 0

Strategic alignment

Stakeholder map, preliminary demand, institutional conversations, regional consultation and international outreach.

Phase 1

Independent feasibility

Energy, connectivity, sites, environment, cooling, demand, cybersecurity, governance, law, impact and financing.

Phase 2

Pilot capability

Modest HPC/AI, initial missions, training, a trusted research environment and operational validation.

Phase 3

National platform

Expanded capacity, university and public access, scientific cloud, national programmes and industry work.

Phase 4

International consortium

Shared science, expanded infrastructure, international funding and regional and global services.

Phase 5

Long-term campus

Possible AI campus, polar-data institute, innovation district, international training and energy laboratory.

13 / PROOF

What still needs to be proven

The vision can proceed only if scientific, territorial, technical and institutional questions are answered with evidence.

  1. What scientific demand exists?
  2. What is the right initial scale?
  3. Which energy model is credible and resilient?
  4. What connectivity redundancy is required?
  5. Which sites meet environmental and logistical requirements?
  6. Which governance model protects public interest?
  7. How will access be allocated?
  8. Which funding model is sustainable?
  9. Which capabilities must remain under Chilean control?
  10. Which international cooperation is mutually beneficial?
  11. How will regional communities and institutions participate?

14 / INSTITUTIONAL INVITATION

Opening a strategic dialogue

Antü Magallanes is opening an exploratory dialogue with institutions capable of contributing scientific knowledge, infrastructure expertise, regional perspective and long-term cooperation.

The immediate purpose is to validate need, form feasibility partnerships, gather expert input and assess interest in a potential consortium. This page neither solicits funding nor implies commitments.

Status and disclaimer

Antü Magallanes is a preliminary concept under development. No final site has been selected; no procurement process exists; no consortium is confirmed; and this document implies no institutional commitment. Participation would require formal agreements. Any infrastructure remains subject to technical, financial, energy, environmental and legal feasibility, permits and applicable consultation.

Named institutions, countries, regions and sectors are potential interlocutors or cooperation directions—not confirmed partners.
